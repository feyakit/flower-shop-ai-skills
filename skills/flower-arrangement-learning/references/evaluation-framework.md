# Bouquet Evaluation Framework

Use this reference for bouquet photo critique, teaching feedback, and edit prompts.

## Source Model

The framework is distilled from the user's Amber Flora commercial floristry textbook and the conversation examples:

- Commercial flower design starts from "color, texture, form".
- Strong work also depends on proportion, scale, focal point, contrast, harmony, unity, line, space, depth, rhythm, and packaging.
- Florist critique should not stop at taste. It should explain what to move, remove, lower, raise, rotate, substitute, or rewrap.

## Diagnosis Dimensions

### 1. Color

Check:

- 主色, 辅助色, 点缀色是否明确.
- Approximate area ratio. 6:3:1 is a useful commercial baseline, not a hard rule.
- Hue relationship: same hue, analogous, medium contrast, complementary, split-complementary, triad, neutral/metallic pairing.
- Value and chroma: too flat, too gray, too saturated, no light/dark rhythm, muddy color from bad packaging.
- Whether wrapping color supports or steals from flowers.

Common defects:

- Too many equal-strength colors with no dominant color.
- Pale flowers placed against pale wrapping with no edge contrast.
- High-chroma accent too large, creating visual noise.
- Packaging color makes flower color look cheap, dirty, or weak.

Fixes:

- Choose one dominant color; reduce accent area.
- Add neutral/green transition material if colors conflict.
- Change wrapping to cream, gray, black, burgundy, or translucent paper depending on mood.
- Use ribbon to echo an existing flower color.

### 2. Flower Form And Material Roles

Classify visually rather than forcing exact species:

- 块状/Round: roses, peonies, dahlias; strong mass and focal weight.
- 面状/Face: flat face flowers; useful for multi-sided work.
- 线状/Line: gladiolus, branches, long leaves; creates direction and negative space.
- 散状/Spray: small branched materials; fills gaps and softens.
- 雾状/Mist: tiny airy clusters; creates haze and lightness.
- 异形/Difficult: orchids, anthuriums, bird-of-paradise; high visual personality.
- 果实/Fruit: adds interest and richness.
- 叶材: background, transition, texture, edge, or massing.

Common defects:

- All materials are same shape, producing a flat pile.
- Too many strong focal flowers compete.
- Line materials shoot in unrelated directions.
- Fillers hide the focal flowers or make the bouquet messy.

Fixes:

- Decide main flower, supporting flower, filler, line, and leaf roles.
- Remove or lower competing focal materials.
- Add one line direction only, or mirror softly with clear intention.
- Use spray/mist materials sparingly to connect gaps.

### 3. Texture

Read texture from visual surface: velvet, silk, glass, leather, cotton, woven/rough, glossy, matte, translucent.

Common defects:

- Flowers and packaging have conflicting textures without a bridge.
- Everything is smooth and soft, making the design bland.
- Rough natural wrapping or raffia makes formal roses look casual and cheap.

Fixes:

- Add a texture bridge: foliage, berries, small branch, or matching ribbon.
- Replace raffia with satin for formal bouquets.
- Replace glossy paper with matte/translucent paper for airy styles.

### 4. Shape, Control, And Proportion

Identify intended control:

- 圆形/closed: symmetrical, full, stable.
- 半开放/open: natural extension, visible negative space.
- 倒梯形 or 倒三角: common Korean/ins bouquet structure.
- 三面观: front and side depth matter.
- 自然风: asymmetry with controlled rhythm, not random looseness.
- 立体花束: strong depth and high-low structure.

Common defects:

- Flowers buried inside wrapping.
- Wrapping is larger than flower face.
- Dome is flat rather than rounded.
- Top-heavy or bottom-clogged.
- One side collapses or extends without balance.

Fixes:

- Raise the flower face above the front opening.
- Make center flowers slightly higher and outer flowers lower/outward for round bouquets.
- Lower front wrapping; keep a controlled higher back only if it supports the design.
- Add front-back depth and high-low changes.

### 5. Focal Point And Visual Movement

Check:

- What catches the eye first?
- Is there one dominant focal zone, or several equal focal points?
- Do lines lead the eye around the bouquet?
- Does the viewer look at flowers first or at paper/ribbon?

Common defects:

- Focal flowers appear at left, center, and right equally.
- A tall vertical element pulls the eye out of the bouquet.
- Large packaging/card/ribbon becomes the real focal point.

Fixes:

- Keep one main focal group and demote others by lowering, rotating, or moving behind.
- Angle line material in one coherent direction.
- Reduce accessory scale.

### 6. Density, Rhythm, And Depth

Check:

- 疏密: where is dense, where is breathable?
- 层次: front/middle/back and high/low differences.
- 节奏: repeated material, changing interval, direction, size, color depth.
- Negative space: whether breathing room is intentional.

Common defects:

- All heads sit on one plane.
- Bottom is packed solid while top shoots out.
- Natural style has random long stems rather than rhythm.
- No visible depth from photo angle.

Fixes:

- Create front low, middle full, back slightly high progression.
- Remove several crowded heads rather than adding more.
- Use 3+ repeated small elements with varied distance/direction to form rhythm.

### 7. Packaging

Packaging should frame and support, not bury or dominate.

Check:

- Paper height: front low enough to reveal flowers, back high only when intentional.
- Edge quality: clean arcs, consistent folds, no messy crushed mouth.
- Material: matches floral mood.
- Volume: bouquet body and paper volume are balanced.
- Binding: ribbon/raffia matches formality.

Common defects:

- Tall paper wall behind a small flower face.
- Front paper covers flowers.
- Inner white paper wrinkles look cheap.
- Raffia too messy for premium romantic flowers.
- Card/logo becomes the focal point.

Fixes:

- Lower paper height by 20-35%.
- Smooth inner folds and create clean low-front/high-back bucket shape.
- Use satin ribbon for formal romance, narrow cream/pink ribbon for gentle bouquets, restrained raffia only for deliberate natural/rustic style.

## Example Critique Patterns

### Pale Pink Bouquet With White Orchid

Likely issues:

- Color is soft and commercial, but flower forms are too similar.
- White orchid creates multiple focal patches.
- Flower face is flat and dense.
- Wrapping may be too large and tall.

Fix:

- Clarify one orchid focal group.
- Raise/lower flowers to build dome.
- Add minimal line or mist material.
- Reduce wrapping height.

### Purple-White Natural Bouquet

Likely issues:

- Good color relationship and richer forms.
- Purple focal flowers may be scattered.
- Long line materials may shoot in unrelated directions.
- Dense white base can clog the lower half.
- Hard paper board may fight the natural line.

Fix:

- Keep one main purple focal zone.
- Shorten or align lines toward one movement.
- Remove crowded base flowers for negative space.
- Soften/lower packaging and refine ribbon.

### Red Rose Bouquet With Cream Textured Paper

Likely issues:

- Roses have strong emotional value, but are buried.
- Wrapping dominates.
- Rose heads are too flat and low.
- Rough raffia conflicts with formal red roses.
- Inner paper edge is messy.

Fix:

- Raise rose face above the front opening.
- Build rounded dome: center slightly higher, outer heads lower and angled outward.
- Lower back paper and front paper.
- Replace raffia with burgundy/cream satin ribbon.
- Keep textured paper only if tidy and less dominant.

## Improved Image Prompt Pattern

When editing/generating a modified bouquet image, use a prompt like:

```text
Edit the provided bouquet photo into an improved professional florist version while preserving the same camera angle, background, hand-held bouquet pose, color family, and core flower identity. Apply these fixes: [specific fixes]. Make the bouquet proportions balanced, flower face clearly visible, focal point coherent, depth and rhythm improved, packaging supportive not dominant, paper edges clean, ribbon/materials matching the style. Photorealistic, natural lighting, no extra text or watermark.
```

Add concrete style details:

- For red roses: classic romantic premium, rounded dome, burgundy or cream satin ribbon.
- For pale pink bouquets: gentle luxury, airy dome, one orchid focal group, soft matte/translucent wrapping.
- For natural purple-white bouquets: clean cool natural style, coherent right-up or left-up line movement, breathable white base, softened wrapping.

## Response Calibration

- Start with the intended style and what works; it makes critique feel grounded.
- Limit major defects to 4-7 points.
- For each defect, include "why it hurts the design" and "what to do".
- End with a one-sentence diagnosis, e.g. "这束不是花不好，是花被包装吃掉了。"
